Denver Health Medical Center (formerly Denver General
Hospital) is the level I trauma center for the city and county
of Denver, and as the Rocky Mountain
regional trauma
center extends service to Colorado and surrounding regions.
During the 18-year period ending December 1996, 312 patients with penetrating neck wounds violating the platysma
muscle anterior to the sternocleidomastoid
muscle were admitted and managed prospectively
according to our treatment algorithm
(Figure). One author (EEM) has maintained a list of these patients since 1978. This series includes
188 patients previously reported,5 and 124 patients subsequently treated in the past 6 years. Patients who died in the
emergency department
(ED) were excluded from this review. The 276 male (88%) and 36 female patients ranged
in age from 15 months to 83 years, with a mean of 3 1 years.
The injury mechanism was stabbing in 233 (75%), gunshot
in 74 (24%), and miscellaneous
in 5 patients. The stab
wounds were created using knives in 199 patients, glass in
22, and other objects (razors, screwdrivers, ice picks, scissors, axe) in 12. The left side was penetrated in 145 patients
(46%), and the right in 104 (33%); in 63 patients (20%)
the wounds were midline or bilateral. The level of penetration was zone I in 41 (13%), zone II in 208 (67%), and
zone III in 63 (20%) patients. Zone I extends from the base
of the neck to the cricoid cartilage, zone II extends from
the cricoid cartilage to the angle of the mandible, and zone
III comprises the area cephalad to the angle of the mandible.
Upon arrival in the ED, management

to
achieve airway patency and tamponade hemorrhage.
Endotracheal
intubation

in patients with expanding hematomas encroaching
on the trachea, as well as those with depressed mental

respiratory compromise, or cardiovascular
collapse. Intravenous access was secured with two large-bore catheters.
